# Drilling-engineer interview and pilot guide

Prepared 4 October 2026, Asia/Jakarta. No interviews or outreach were conducted. Obtain separate authorization before contacting anyone. Do not request confidential well data; use approved anonymized examples.

## Sampling and session structure

Suggested discovery sample: 8–12 people spanning operator planning engineers, operations engineers, directional engineers, contractor/service specialists, independent reviewers and an IT/data owner. Include different deployment settings and well types. This is a proposed exploratory sample, not statistically representative demand validation.

A 45–60 minute session: explain the research-only scope and permission to take notes; reconstruct the last relevant job; inspect approved artefacts; observe a task using the current product; rank pain and alternatives; record disconfirming evidence. Do not start by selling all 17 modules.

## Questions

1. Walk through the last drilling programme or change you prepared. What triggered it, what inputs arrived, and who reviewed it?
2. Which files or systems did you copy information between? Show an approved example of a unit, well-name, datum or version mismatch.
3. Describe the last time a changed survey, mud or casing assumption required other calculations to change. How did you find every dependent result?
4. Show the last alternative comparison. What evidence determined the choice, and which uncertainty could have reversed it?
5. What must a reviewer find in your programme/report? What causes a submission to be returned? Who has actual approval authority?
6. Reconstruct yesterday's activity/DDR process. How are overlapping activities, gaps, NPT, currency and cost corrections handled?
7. Which outputs come from WITSML, rig systems or vendor portals? What versions/objects, permissions, delay and connectivity constraints apply?
8. Which engineering calculations do you use most? Identify one case the current GeoDrill assumptions would exclude and how you solve it today.
9. For surveys and proximity, who supplies the tool error model, CRS/north correction and acceptance rule? What offset data is unavailable?
10. For loads, hydraulics, wear, bit condition or anomaly claims, what independently measured reference would you trust? What data rights and calibration evidence exist?
11. Which lookup in past reports took longest recently? What exact question, source and page would make a retrieved answer verifiable?
12. What happens when a laptop fails or two people edit? What local/network/cloud restrictions and backup requirements must any tool meet?
13. Which current product/workaround would GeoDrill complement or replace for one task? What integration would make switching impossible?
14. Rank your top three repeated problems by frequency and consequence using actual examples. Which proposed upgrade would you remove?
15. Who decides adoption and budget? What evidence, pilot and support obligations are necessary? Ask about the process without implying commitment to purchase.

## Observed tasks and metrics

Task A: import an anonymized source, map aliases/units and identify an intentionally missing datum. Task B: reopen baseline/alternative studies and explain source/model differences. Task C: revise a shared input and identify affected studies. Task D: find the evidence for a chosen programme result, including a withheld calculation. Task E: reconcile a sample activity/cost day if A06 is being tested.

Record time, completed/abandoned steps, wrong interpretations, requested help, evidence lookup success and unit/version mistakes. A successful task does not show physical model validity. Compare task performance with the participant's approved existing workflow; counterbalance order when possible.

## Hypothesis and decision log

Use H1–H10 from REPORT.md. For each note, retain role/segment, recent factual example, frequency, current workaround, observed consequence, contrary evidence and confidence. Distinguish participant statement, observation and researcher inference.

Move a feature ahead only when repeated examples and observed tasks support it in the intended segment. Do not convert eight opinions into market-share or ROI claims. Record unresolved data access and qualification gates explicitly.
